Project overview

An inspectable pipeline, end to end

NeuroScan AI is an academic research prototype. It classifies a single brain MRI slice into four trained categories, saves the result against a patient record, and produces a transparent report with the original image, the Grad-CAM heatmap, and the Grad-CAM overlay.

01

Real inference, no mock results

Every prediction comes from the existing VGG16 weights in backend/model. Uploaded images are resized to 128×128 with bilinear interpolation and normalized to [0, 1]. The interface never fabricates a probability or a heatmap.

02

Explainability by construction

Grad-CAM gradients are taken from block5_conv3 for the predicted class. The raw Jet-colormapped activation map and the 55/45 blended overlay are both returned and stored with the scan.

03

Persistent patient records

Patients are registered in SQLite and every scan is linked to a patient ID, a unique scan ID, a report reference, four stored probabilities, and the generated report. History searches query that database directly.

04

Measured, not claimed

The reported 90.94% accuracy and 69.25% glioma recall were measured on all 1,600 held-out test images. The limitations section states where the model fails, including the glioma-to-meningioma confusion.

Explainability

How Grad-CAM is computed

  1. Forward pass

    The uploaded slice is propagated through the VGG16 backbone and classifier head.

  2. Gradient capture

    Gradients of the predicted class score are taken with respect to block5_conv3 feature maps.

  3. Channel weighting

    Spatial gradients are averaged per channel and used to weight the activation maps.

  4. Visualisation

    The rectified map is normalized, resized to the upload, colormapped, and blended with the original at 55% MRI / 45% heatmap.

The heatmap shows where the network’s gradient is strongest. It is not a segmentation mask, a lesion boundary, or a radiological finding.

Workflow

From upload to archived report

Every step below runs locally against the Flask backend and the project’s SQLite database.

01

Patient intake

Enter a patient ID and demographics, or verify an existing record. Real IDs are generated by the backend.

02

Image validation

JPG, JPEG, or PNG up to 16 MB. The browser previews the exact file that will be sent.

03

Preprocessing

RGB conversion, 128×128 bilinear resize, and [0, 1] normalization exactly as in the inference module.

04

Inference

VGG16 produces four softmax probabilities. The highest class and its confidence are recorded.

05

Grad-CAM

The class-specific activation map and blended overlay are generated from the same forward pass data.

06

Report & archive

The scan, probabilities, images, and self-contained HTML report are persisted under the patient record.

Limitations

Read before interpreting a result

  • Single-slice input. The model sees one 128×128 image, not a full volumetric study or clinical context.
  • Four classes only. Other tumours, infections, artefacts, and normal variants are outside the label space.
  • Weak glioma recall. 69.25% of glioma test images were detected; the rest were mostly called meningioma.
  • Dataset-bound. Performance is measured on the project test split and has not been externally validated.
  • Explainability is not segmentation. Grad-CAM highlights gradient-weighted regions, not tumour boundaries.
  • Not a medical device. The system has no regulatory clearance and must not guide patient care.
